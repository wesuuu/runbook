# The chat assistant

Batchrite includes a built-in AI assistant called **Trellis AI**. It can answer questions about Batchrite itself ("how do I create a protocol?"), help you search and reason over your organisation's uploaded library documents, and draft protocol structures on request. You just ask in plain language — the assistant determines what kind of question it is and responds accordingly.

## What you can do

- Ask questions about how Batchrite works and get a direct, cited answer.
- Search your organisation's library documents by asking a science or procedural question — the assistant reads relevant passages and attributes its answer to the source.
- Draft a protocol outline by describing the process you have in mind.
- Use skill chips to focus the assistant on a specific task before you start typing.
- Review source citations attached to each response to verify where the information came from.

## How to ask a question

The assistant is available in two ways: as a floating panel accessible from any page, and as a full-page view.

**Using the floating panel:**

1. Click the chat bubble button in the bottom-right corner of any page to open the panel, or press **Cmd+J** (Mac) / **Ctrl+J** (Windows/Linux) from anywhere in the app.
2. Type your question in the **Ask a question...** box at the bottom of the panel.
3. Press **Enter** (or **Shift+Enter** for a new line) to send.
4. The assistant replies in the panel. If its answer draws on a library document, small source chips appear below the response showing the document title and page number. Click a chip to open that document at the referenced section.

**Using the full-page view:**

1. In the top navigation bar, click **AI Chat**.
2. Click **Start a conversation** to begin, or select an existing conversation from the **Chats** sidebar on the left.
3. Type in the message box at the bottom and press **Enter** to send.
4. If the response is based on library documents, a **N sources** link appears below the message. Click it to open a **Sources** panel on the right, which lists each cited document with a snippet and a link to the exact section.

The assistant briefly shows what it is doing while it works — for example, it notes when it searches your documents or looks up available unit operations. These activity notes appear in small italic text above the response.

If a response seems to be taking too long or was interrupted, the assistant offers a **Resend** option to retry, or **Keep waiting** if you prefer to let it finish.

## How skills work

Skills are focused modes that direct the assistant toward a specific type of task. When a chat panel or full page is first opened, available skills appear as a row of clickable buttons below the input field. Once a conversation is underway, the same skills are accessible from the wrench icon (the tools menu) next to the input field.

Clicking a skill activates it. A small chip appears above the input field showing the skill name, and the placeholder text changes to prompt you to ask within that skill's scope. Type your question and send as normal. To remove the active skill and return to the default mode, click the **×** on the skill chip.

Skills are configured by your organisation's administrator. The set of available skills, and what each one does, depends on your organisation's setup.

## Product questions vs. document questions

The assistant handles two distinct types of questions, but you do not need to choose — it routes automatically.

- **Product questions** ("how do I start a run?", "where is the protocol editor?") are answered from Batchrite's built-in knowledge of the application. The assistant describes the relevant steps and features directly.
- **Document questions** ("what is the recommended seeding density in our SOP?", "does our process manual mention buffer pH?") are answered by searching your organisation's library. The assistant reads the relevant passages and cites the source documents so you can verify the information.

You can mix both types in the same conversation. Just ask, and the assistant responds from the right source.
